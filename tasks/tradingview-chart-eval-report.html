An evaluation of the charting goal against TradingView's actual product lineup, Radon's data APIs, and IBKR's instrument + market-data realities. Led by the constraints most likely to kill the project, then debated through three adversarial passes.

Premise correction. The screenshot is the TradingView web app, which is not embeddable. What you can integrate is one of five different products, and the one that recreates that screenshot (Advanced Charts / Trading Platform) carries a free license that is public-websites-only and likely excludes an auth-gated app like Radon. The two gates (you didn't mention either)

Gate 1 — TradingView license vs Radon's shape. Their FAQ states verbatim: "according to the license agreement you can use the library on public websites only." Radon sits behind Clerk auth and is effectively a private operator tool. Standard reading: auth-gated / internal / paid apps need a negotiated commercial license. high that the restriction exists; moderate on the Radon-specific ruling (get it in writing). Enterprise price is not public unknown.
Gate 2 — IBKR market-data redistribution. IBKR real-time data is licensed to you the subscriber; exchanges (Nasdaq, NYSE/CTA, OPRA) restrict external display. A hosted browser receiving IBKR-sourced real-time prices is architecturally redistribution. high it's restricted; moderate on single-user enforcement. Escape hatches: delayed (15-min) data, a licensed vendor, or device-local-only real-time.

Three adversarial passes

Round 1 — Plan A: Advanced/Trading Platform, IBKR-fed

Advanced Charts (Trading Platform tier) on the detail page; custom JS Datafeed on FastAPI; real-time from the :8765 relay; earnings marks via getMarks; on-chart buy/sell wired to IBKR via the Broker API.

  • License invalid for Radon's shape — behind Clerk = not a public website. Breaches the free license on day one. high
  • Broker API duplicates and threatens the order chokepoint. Radon's rule is every order through OrderRiskGate + Four Gates + net-of-cost computeOrderRisk. TradingView's placeOrder is a parallel path that bypasses your gates. high
  • IBKR is a poor backfill source — reqHistoricalData pacing (~60 req/10min, duration caps) + shallow intraday depth. A chart scrolling years of 1m bars hits the wall instantly. high (exact numbers mod)
  • Redistribution (Gate 2). high
  • Theming/iframe friction — Advanced Charts loads in an iframe and themes via an overrides object, not var(--token). Your "no raw hex" rule does not pass through. mod-high

Round 2 — Plan B: decouple, delay data, no on-chart trading

Advanced Charts only if licensed, else fall back to Lightweight Charts + a hand-built toolbar. Default hosted chart to delayed/EOD data (Yahoo + a vendor) cached in Turso; IBKR real-time device-local only. Keep the chart read-only; deep-link the existing ticket.

  • You deleted the reason the screenshot is impressive. Lightweight Charts has none of the 110 tools / 100 indicators / 13 types / marks. The fallback is "build a charting platform," a multi-quarter effort. The Ferrari became a bicycle with a weld-it-yourself kit. high
  • Delayed data guts a trading terminal. Radon is "flow signal or nothing," sub-500ms. A 15-min-delayed chart contradicts the product. Legality and purpose are in direct tension; Plan B resolves it by neutering. high

Round 3 — The reframe that resolves it

What data is the chart actually showing? Your screenshot shows MU at $1,211.38, June 2026, +300% YTD — not real (high). That is Radon's synthetic / reconstructed series. That single fact reorders everything:

  • If the chart renders Radon's own computed series (reconstructed structure, forecast bands, your normalized OHLCV), it is genuinely "your data." Gate 2 largely evaporates and you fit Advanced Charts' "your servers, your data" model cleanly. mod-high, contingent on provenance.
  • If it must show raw real-time exchange prices, no library saves you; you need delayed data or a licensed vendor regardless.

So the real first task is not picking a library. It is answering: is the detail-page chart showing licensed real-time, delayed, or Radon-derived data? That answer selects the path.

Gaps

UI / UX

  • The right-hand watchlist + symbol-info + key-stats panel is not part of any TradingView library — those are separate web-app widgets. Radon builds it from data it already has (watchlist.json, UW key stats, FU3/FU4). high
  • Brand-token theming through iframe/overrides needs a token→overrides translation layer (no raw hex, 4px radius). high
  • Drawing/layout persistence must be server-side (Turso) to survive across machines. high
  • Mobile — Advanced Charts is heavy and iframe-mounted; it fights the PWA shell + App Router. Lightweight Charts is far better on mobile, which reintroduces the two-renderer cost. mod-high
  • Tick→bar aggregation for the live candle is real work and a classic bug source. mod

APIs

  • price_history_cache (capped ~500) is not a bar store. Need a per-symbol/per-resolution OHLCV table + gap-fill + backfill pipeline. high
  • No clean deep intraday source today — Yahoo intraday is shallow/unofficial; budget Polygon/Databento/Tiingo. IBKR pacing makes it unusable as primary backfill. high
  • Real-time bridge from the relay into subscribeBars does not exist (relay is built for depth/montage, not chart bars). high
  • getMarks plumbing for earnings/dividends/flow must be added (data exists post-FU3/FU4; endpoint does not). mod
  • Broker API ↔ OrderRiskGate adapter does not exist and is the most dangerous piece (parallel order path). high

Instruments IBKR supports vs what the chart demands

IBKR covers stocks, ETFs, options, futures, futures options, FX cash, cash indices, bonds, funds, warrants, CFDs, metals, crypto (Paxos) high. Radon's relay today: stocks, options, index futures (VIX→VX/CFE, SPX→ES, NDX→NQ, RUT→RTY). Datafeed gaps:

  • Futures — IBKR gives single contracts; charting wants a continuous front-month series. You build roll logic. high
  • FX — no equity volume (has_volume=false), 5-dp pricescale, 24×5 sessions. high
  • Cash indices (SPX, VIX as index) — separate CBOE entitlement from the futures you route to now. mod-high
  • Options — OCC symbology + near-useless historical bars for illiquid strikes; out of scope for v1. high
  • Crypto / bonds / funds — sparse or non-chartable; exclude. high
  • For a page called "stock detail," the 80% case (stocks/ETFs) is clean. The trap: one FX/futures ticker with a wrong SymbolInfo renders garbage, so resolveSymbol must fail safe.

Bottom line

The engineering is the easy 30%. The project is gated by (1) the TradingView license ruling for an auth-gated app, (2) your market-data display rights, and (3) the provenance of the data you intend to chart. Answer those three first; they decide whether you ship Advanced Charts with your flow overlays (the version worth building) or a stripped Lightweight Charts widget. Keep trading off the chart until it can route through OrderRiskGate, or you quietly build a second, ungated path to your live account.
