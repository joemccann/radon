Radon · Feature Assessment

Leveraging a decoder-only time-series foundation model

Three candidate features built on Google Research's TimesFM, with the honest case against naïve use first.

Source: research.google/blog/a-decoder-only-foundation-model-for-time-series-forecasting · Assessment date 2026-06-20

Read this before building anything

TimesFM is a strong model trained on traffic, weather, energy demand, Wikipedia pageviews, Google Trends — series with heavy autocorrelation, stable seasonality, high signal-to-noise. Financial price/return series are the adversarial worst case for exactly this class of model. Returns are near-martingale, non-stationary, regime-switching, fat-tailed. Point a foundation forecaster at SPY prices and trade the point forecast, and you lose money slowly then quickly.

The rule for every feature below: never forecast price or direction. Forecast the structured, mean-reverting, autocorrelated series radon already computes — vol, flow volumes, breadth, regime indices — and treat the forecast residual (actual minus expected) as the edge, or the quantile spread as the risk input. That is where the model earns its keep and maps cleanly onto the Four Gates.

Why the architecture matters here

Asymmetric patchingInput patch 32 → output patch 128. One forward pass yields a long horizon from short context. Cheap intraday→EOD / day→week, no autoregressive drift.
Zero-shotNo per-ticker training. Run across the entire watchlist nightly on a modest budget.
Quantile headsShipped checkpoint emits quantiles (0.1–0.9), not just the point MAE the blog highlights. This is the single most useful output for Gates 1 & 3 — it gives a distribution.
200M params · 100B time-pointsSmall, fast, broadly pre-trained. TimesFM 2.0 extends context to ~2048 and adds covariate support (condition flow on VIX / day-type).

Feature 1 — Regime-index forecast layer

build #2

Forecast bands on CRI / VCG / GEX / VIX-term

// forecast your own structural state variables, not price

Forecasts

The regime indices radon already persists to Turso and charts (/regime/*). Smooth, autocorrelated, mean-reverting — TimesFM's home turf.

Ships

A forecast band (median + 0.1/0.9 quantiles) extending each regime chart 5–20 sessions forward, using the established regime-chart convention (BrushMinimap + presets). A nightly systemd unit runs zero-shot inference and writes a regime_forecast table.

Serves

Timing for Gate 2 — Edge. "CRI forecast to cross the top decile within 4 sessions, 0.9 band already there" is actionable and falsifiable. Predicts structural state, not price.

Risk

Abrupt jumps (gaps, holidays) get smoothed over and missed. Display as conditional-on-no-shock; overlay the residual so a violent miss is itself the signal.

Confidence
High — works as a feature   Moderate — beats a damped-trend baseline

Feature 2 — Flow-volume nowcast + residual anomaly detector

build #1

Expected vs. actual flow ("flow surprise")

// the edge is the residual, not the forecast

Forecasts

The expected baseline for each ticker's dark-pool / OTC metrics — DPI, print volume, sweep counts, premium — conditioned on day-of-week, level, seasonality. Count-like seasonal series, structurally identical to the "demand forecasting" TimesFM was benchmarked on.

The edge

Replace hand-tuned z-score thresholds with: flag tickers where today's flow exceeds the TimesFM 0.9-quantile expectation. "Unusual" becomes "in excess of a 100B-point-trained seasonal baseline." Calendar-seasonal spikes stop false-firing; genuine anomalies on quiet names surface louder.

Ships

Augment scan/discover and the VCG/GEX caches with expected_flow and a flow_surprise score (actual ÷ forecast-quantile). Re-rank the watchlist by surprise. A re-ranking of existing surfaces, not a new page.

Serves

Directly sharpens Gate 2 — a specific dark-pool signal that hasn't moved price. Excess flow vs. seasonal baseline, before price reacts, is precisely that. Defensive: kills absolute-threshold false positives.

Risk

Flow is spiky and event-driven; TimesFM under-forecasts tails — helps here (bigger residuals) but means nominal quantile calibration is off. You must recalibrate the surprise threshold on your own data; don't trust the nominal 0.9.

Confidence
Moderate-to-high — mechanism sound, calibration is the work

Feature 3 — Probabilistic expected-move cone

build #3

Quantile vol distribution feeding convexity + Kelly

// distribution in, not direction

Forecasts

The distribution of near-term realized volatility (secondarily, the underlying's quantile cone). NOT a directional point forecast.

Why quantiles

Gate 1 (convexity, gain ≥ 2× loss) and Gate 3 (fractional Kelly, 2.5% cap) both need a distribution over outcomes. TimesFM's quantile output over realized vol gives a forward expected-move cone in one forward pass — a zero-shot, no-fit alternative / ensemble partner to garch-convergence.

Ships

A forecast_cone in the evaluate [TICKER] pipeline and the order-builder risk surface. The existing OrderRiskGate chokepoint consumes the distribution → P(max gain), P(max loss), Kelly-implied size. Render the cone on the eval chart.

Serves

Turns Gates 1 & 3 from heuristics into distributional math, plus a GARCH cross-check — agreement raises conviction, divergence flags.

Sharpest risk

Do not let this become a directional price forecaster in disguise. Vol forecasting is defensible (mean-reverting). The price cone is a width estimate only; its center is noise and must be treated as zero, or it silently violates "never rationalize a bad trade." Intraday microstructure is out-of-distribution — prefer daily bars, longer horizons.

Confidence
Moderate — vol width   Low — any price-center signal (treat center as zero)

Build order

  1. Feature 2 (flow residual) — most aligned with "flow signal or nothing," reuses existing scan surfaces, residual framing is robust even when the model is mediocre.
  2. Feature 1 (regime forecast band) — cleanest fit to the model's strengths, lowest risk, mostly a charting addition.
  3. Feature 3 (expected-move cone) — highest payoff if it works, highest risk of misuse; gate it behind a backtest vs. GARCH.
Cross-cutting demand

Backtest before shipping any of them. The quant-backtest skill exists for this. For each feature the bar is beating a dumb baseline — seasonal-naïve for flow, AR / damped-trend for regime, GARCH for vol. A 200M zero-shot model that ties a one-line baseline is not worth a systemd unit.

High at least one of the three clears that bar.   Low that all three do. Build Feature 2, prove it, then expand.

Correction to the blog's framing: it implies point-estimates only — the shipped TimesFM checkpoint does emit quantiles, and 2.0 extends context to ~2048 with covariate support. Verify the current checkpoint's quantile calibration yourself; do not take nominal levels on faith.
